Context

A feature only creates value if people can use it. Most of our users are HR teams and hiring managers, not software specialists, and when they're stuck they need a clear answer they can find on their own.

Problem

Product knowledge lived in people's heads and call recordings. Some features had articles, some had outdated ones, and many new features shipped with none.

The same questions kept arriving on WhatsApp and email: adding a team, posting to LinkedIn, sending an offer. Each took someone's time, and the answer was lost in a chat.

We also wanted an AI assistant in the product to answer these questions. An assistant is only as good as its source material, and there was no complete, reliable source for it to use.

Goal

One complete, current source of answers for every feature, clear enough for customers to use directly and structured well enough for our AI assistant to answer from.

What I built

  1. An article for every feature, organised by task: company and team setup, posting jobs, reviewing candidates, interviews, offers, the HR system integration, and AI.
  2. Role-based getting-started guides for admins and recruiters, and for hiring managers and interviewers.
  3. A consistent format: steps on the left, screenshot on the right, one task per article.
  4. Written for HR users in plain language. Clear, specific answers turned out to be exactly what the AI needed too.
  5. Documentation as part of every release. No feature ships without its article and a changelog entry.

Powering the AI assistant

The help centre became the knowledge base for Whitecarrot AI, our in-product assistant. When a customer asks how to set up booking links, it answers from the help centre and links to the full article.

To keep it improving, I run a feedback loop:

  1. Find the gaps. Each week we review the assistant's logs for questions it couldn't answer, or answered poorly.
  2. Close them. I write the missing answer into the right article, or correct the one that was wrong.
  3. The assistant improves. The next customer with that question gets an accurate answer, and so does anyone reading the help centre.

The logs double as a product signal. When many users ask how to do the same thing, the feature itself is often too hard to use, and that goes into the backlog.

Impact

About 80 articles in 10 sectionscovering the whole product, including Whitecarrot AI and AI headhunting, published as those features launched.
The foundation for Whitecarrot AIThe assistant answers customers' "how do I" questions from this help centre. It's part of our top plan, so answer quality directly supports that plan's value.
Lower support loadEvery question answered by an article or the assistant is one the team doesn't handle manually.

Success metrics: the assistant's resolution rate (questions answered without a person stepping in), and how it improves as the weekly loop closes gaps.

Learnings

  • Good documentation and a good AI assistant need the same thing: clear, specific, current answers.
  • Once AI answers from your help centre, an outdated article doesn't just fail to help. It produces a wrong answer, so keeping it current is product work.
  • Writing the article tests the feature. If it takes ten steps to explain, the feature probably needs simplifying.
